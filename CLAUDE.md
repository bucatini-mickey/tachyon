# Tachyon: handoff notes

Tachyon is Mickey's game-agnostic engine for Dwarf-Fortress-style ASCII games in the terminal. It started in a claude.ai chat on 2026-10-07, and these notes carry that context over.

## Goal
- One engine, several games: **Broadside** (naval, in `../Broadside`, Python, used as inspiration only), a **gothic** Morrowind-like RPG (hell, demons, cults, hallucinations), a **desert** Mad-Max-like wanderer (isolation, a massive world), and maybe a **spy** game. The engine knows about none of them.
- DF-style: the world is a 3D grid of 1 m cells, shown one z-level at a time (usually the player's). A cell is drawn as its most important thing; a table with items on it still reads as a table, slightly tinted.
- Real per-cell physics: liquid in 64ths that flows and drains to 0, heat, gas, light and fire. Walls hide what's behind them from the player's eyes.
- Fast above all ("Tachyon").

## Decisions
- **Rust**, with **crossterm as the only dependency**. Mickey is comfortable with Python and is learning Rust with Claude's help, so explain Rust idioms when they come up. He wants a barebones setup that is easy to install, build and package, Linux-friendly, and able to reach macOS and phones (WASM) later.
- **The renderer is separate from the terminal.** Everything draws into `frame::Frame`; only `term.rs` and `app.rs` touch crossterm. A web, phone or native backend only has to display a `Frame` and send `input::KeyEvent`s.
- The world is stored structure-of-arrays (one `Vec` per property). It is split into 16x16x1 chunks, and only chunks touched recently get simulated (`world::Wake`). A slow rolling sweep wakes sleeping chunks now and then.
- Materials, including liquids and gases, live in one registry (`material.rs`). `Materials::standard()` is an optional starter set; games add their own.

## Layout
```
src/
  color geom rng noise       utilities (wyrand RNG, value noise)
  material tile world        cell grid: Tile shape + material + fluid/gas/temp/fire/sky arrays
  things                     items/furniture/creatures; generational slot map + spatial index; Layer draw priority
  clock scene movement       time of day; Scene = world+things+clock+sim; walking, ramps, stairs, doors, swimming, falling
  sim/ fluid gas heat light  cellular automata over awake chunks; sky exposure; LightMap (coloured flood fill)
  view/ mod fov inspect      MapView: z-slice render, look-down depth, FOV + memory, lighting, overlays; cell description
  frame input term app       cell buffer (+ to_html/to_text), diffed true-colour terminal output, Game trait + main loop
  vessel                     rafts/boats: rigid groups of deck things floating on the liquid
  weather projectile         Weather -> World::wind/rain each tick; Projectile + explode() (Blast)
  atlas blueprint worldgen   authored world map (text format); structures (text format); atlas -> 1 m World
  sea craft ballistics sight the open sea as fields (tide, currents, waves, whirlpools); ships as scenes posed on it; round shot; seeing over the horizon
  life/ body needs mind ai path species   creatures: tissue-layer bodies + wounds, needs, minds/routines, A* paths
examples/editor/             world editor: atlas painting + 1 m building + blueprints (palette.rs holds biome/rock/furniture defs)
examples/seaway/             a brig on a modelled sea: helm, sails, broadsides, chart, lookout
examples/sandbox/            test map (house, cellar, lava cave, lakes, canal + sluice + bridge, raft, tower, hut, well, pit, trees, villagers)
```

## Status (2026-10-07, Claude Code session)
- Rust 1.99 (MSVC) installed on Rosemary via winget/rustup; VS 2022 Build Tools were already present.
- Sandbox builds and runs. `cargo test --release` passes (8 tests in `tests/physics.rs`). README.md written.
- Timings (160x100x12): ~2.0 ms/tick, ~1.1 ms to draw 200x60; ~3 ms/tick while the hut burns. `--bench` prints a per-system breakdown and, with `--fire`, what burned.
- Fixed from testing:
  - heat: changes under 0.001 C were dropped on write-back, which leaked heat. Heat crossing into a sleeping chunk now goes into an `edge` list and is handed over, and only wakes the chunk if it is >0.5 C in one tick (`WAKE_JUMP`).
  - fluid: slopes of 1 unit per cell never levelled. Trickle now calls `seek_low`, an 8-step downhill walk; pools level to within 2/64.
  - fire never spread, because air losses keep the next wood cell under 300 C. Added `Material::spread`, a chance per tick to light a random neighbour: wood 0.1, leaves 0.2, grass 0.04 (grass fires die out). The hut burns down in about 1000 ticks.
- 2026-10-08: walking and boats.
  - `input::DirPad`: a tap is one step, holding walks (220 ms, then 110 ms per step), and two held arrows walk diagonally. It relies on key-release events: Windows reports them natively, kitty-protocol terminals get them via `PushKeyboardEnhancementFlags`, and anywhere else it falls back to one step per press. `Game` gained `on_key_up` and `update` (called every frame).
  - Fluid flow field: `World::flow_x/flow_y` hold the smoothed net flux (`FLOW_KEEP` 0.85). `World::current(p)` is that flux divided by depth, in cells per tick. `World::surface` gives the water height; `take_fluid` removes liquid; `World::wind` sets the wind.
  - `sim::Source`: a positive rate is a constant-head spring (keeps its cell full, never overflows); a negative rate is a drain (its cell, then up to 2 below).
  - `vessel::Vessel` (stored in `Scene::vessels`, stepped in `Scene::tick`) floats at the surface with hysteresis, is dragged toward the current under its wet parts, takes wind and `push` impulses, stops at walls and shallows, carries what is on its deck, and pushes water from its bow to its sides and front. The wake conserves water exactly. Things with `ThingKind::deck` can be stood on (movement handles climbing aboard from the water).
  - Sandbox: a canal runs from the west lake to a sluice (springs at the mouth, drains at the end), with a plank bridge and a 3x3 raft. Walking off the raft paddles (`PADDLE` 0.025); `J` jumps overboard; `W` cycles the wind.
  - The fluid CA has no pressure, so liquid only moves sideways at the surface. Currents come from surface slopes: a canal reaches ~1 m/s, a deep lake is still. 13 tests pass. ~3.5 ms/tick, since the flowing canal keeps its chunks awake.
- 2026-10-08 (later): elements, weather, firebolt.
  - Flames on things: `ThingKind::flame` is `Flame::Exposed` (torch, candle) or `Sheltered` (lantern, brazier). Things have `lit` and `wet`. `sim::flames::step` (run in `Scene::tick`) handles: liquid >= 16 puts a flame out and soaks it; under open sky, rain soaks and puts out exposed flames, and gusts over 0.7 cells/tick blow them out; wet things dry (faster when warm); fire or >250 C relights a dry flame. `flames::try_light` gets harder the wetter a thing is. Unlit flames give no light and draw charred.
  - `weather::Weather` (on `Scene`) turns the prevailing wind_speed/wind_dir/rain into `World::wind` (gusts from value noise, direction veers) and `World::rain` each tick. Wind drifts gas under open sky, fans exposed fires (faster spread, biased downwind), blows out torches and pushes vessels. Rain adds water drops (`RAIN_DROPS`), douses exposed fires and is drawn as streaks along the wind. New gas `mist` (does not rise; lingers) is used for fog.
  - `projectile::explode`: force `power * (1 - d/(r+1))` minus the `Material::strength` of the walls in between, so stone shelters what is behind it. Walls weaker than the force break (to rubble on solid ground, otherwise to open air); floors hanging over open space break at 1.5x; ground over a broken wall caves in. Heat, ignition chance, smoke, and non-creature things over 60 force are destroyed (dry flames relight). `Projectile` moves a thing in sub-steps; it bursts before a wall or floor, at a creature or blocking thing, or at its range; it fizzles into steam in liquid >= 32. `projectile::trace` gives the aiming line, drawn with `MapView::marks`. `Scene` has projectiles, short-lived `effects` (the flash), and `events` for the game to drain. New `Layer::Effect` draws above creatures.
  - Sandbox: `f` aims a firebolt (paused, cursor plus aiming line; Enter throws). The bolt is power 120, radius 2.5, ignite 1.0: it holes a wood wall, lights the edges, and the hut burns down in about 900 ticks. `R` cycles rain, `F` brings fog, `W` cycles the prevailing wind. Two characters per cell is now the default (`--narrow` for snapshots). 20 tests pass. ~3.5 ms/tick, ~7 ms in heavy rain and a gale.
  - The user's screenshot of the canal "glitching" looked like tool-placed torches still burning underwater, plus lava and obsidian lighting. That is now prevented by the flame rules.
- Looking up: where the camera level is a `WallStyle::Solid` wall (natural ground), `MapView::above` draws the surface 1..=`up_depth` (2) levels above it, hazed per level (`UP_HAZE`). Built walls and deep rock keep the occluded look. Visibility uses extra FOVs on the open levels over the observer's head (`fov_up`; stops at any ceiling). `U` toggles it; `--noup` is the snapshot flag. Draw is ~1.5 ms.
- 2026-10-09 fixes:
  - `World::open_sky` means nothing at all overhead (computed with `sky`). It is now used for rain, wind, gas drift and rain on flames and fires. `sky` still leaks sideways through doors and windows (light, outdoor air temperature), so it had made it rain just inside doorways.
  - `World::quench(i)` puts out a fire, cools it to 100 C (`QUENCH_TEMP`) and puffs steam. Water in the cell, water against a burning wall (chance 0.25/tick; not lava), and rain all quench. Before this, dousing and rain didn't cool the cell, so it relit the next tick, and walls couldn't be put out at all. The sandbox `w` tool throws water over a burning wall.
  - Diagonals: the `DirPad` chord window is now 70 ms, and a key joining a held one steps within the chord window. On Windows, `term.rs` synthesises key-ups with `GetAsyncKeyState` (user32, no new crate) when the terminal sends none. The sandbox panel shows "key releases from: terminal / keyboard poll / none yet". 22 tests pass.
- 2026-10-09: world creation. Mickey wants to be the primary author of some worlds (e.g. the vampire/cult game). Procedural generation will come later and write into the same atlas, perhaps science-based (erosion, river abrasion).
  - `atlas::Atlas`: w x h cells of `REGION` (32) m, each with elevation (m above sea, i16), rock and biome (palette indices into name lists), plus `rivers` (atlas-cell polylines; the first point is the source) and `features` (world-metre x, y; z in m above sea or `GROUND`; kind, e.g. "town" or "bp:<name>"; label). `elevation_at` is Catmull-Rom. Saved as text by `to_text` / `from_text`.
  - `blueprint::Blueprint`: a box of cells (None = keep), each with tile, material name and thing-kind names. `capture` / `stamp` / text with a per-file key (one character per cell; furniture uses its initial). Creatures are never captured.
  - `worldgen::generate(atlas, GenSettings, rect)` gives a `Generated`: a World, `sea_z` (the floor level of elevation 0; the sea is full below it), sources (river springs across the width at the source, drains across the width at the exit, sea-edge hold springs and overflow drains) and features with local positions. Heights are interpolated plus biome-scaled noise (damped near sea level). Biome and rock borders are jittered. Steep slopes (>=3) show rock. River beds descend monotonically and are cut below the lowest bank within the width + 1. Puddles only go on level ground. The same atlas and seed always give the same world. Biomes are `BiomeDef` (surface or None for bare rock, subsoil, depth, trees, roughness, puddles).

- Rendering fix (2026-10-09): the "glitching elements" in Mickey's screenshots (the canal, and the rainy sandbox with a mangled panel) were cursor drift in `term::present`. It skipped cursor moves for runs of adjacent cells, assuming every glyph is one column, but terminals and fonts draw some non-ASCII glyphs two columns wide. Now only ASCII advances the cursor implicitly; after a non-ASCII glyph the next cell gets an explicit move and is always redrawn. Ctrl+L repaints everything in any app. If artifacts remain, find which glyph the terminal draws wide and swap it.
- 2026-10-10: creatures (`src/life/`). A creature = a Thing on the creature layer + a `Creature` in `scene.life` (`Life`), keyed by ThingId. `Life::step` runs at the end of `Scene::tick`, driven by game seconds (`clock.seconds()` delta), so skipping time ages needs and wounds.
  - body.rs: `BodyPlan` = tree of `PartDef`s (parent, `inside` for organs, size = hit weight, `Height` band, tissue layers outside-in with mm, `Does` flags THINK/PUMP/BREATHE/STAND/GRASP/SEE/BITE/LIMB/VITAL, `vascular`). `humanoid(mass)` and `quadruped(mass, coat, tail)`; thickness and toughness scale with cbrt(mass/70). `Body::strike(plan, part, DamageKind, force)` goes layer by layer (edge cuts, point pierces and glances off bone, blunt bruises/fractures/shakes organs, burn sears skin) and on into an organ; edge through a LIMB severs it and its children. Wounds merge per (kind, tissue), bleed (scaled by blood volume and vascular), clot (`TissueProps::clot` x vascular), hurt, cost function, heal per day. Bruise colour by age. `capacities()` gives conscious/moving/grasp/sight/breathing/pumping/biting/pain/blood. `fatal()`: vital severed or destroyed, blood < 40%, burns over 60% of the body.
  - needs.rs: hunger/thirst/fatigue per day, core temperature drifting with air outside the species' comfort range (wet feels 8 C colder), breath under water. `factor()` scales consciousness; `fatal()` drowned/froze/heatstroke/thirst/starved.

Every 60 ticks or when a goal ends: thirst/hunger/sleep scored, then routine, then idle/wander near home. Same-kind goals are kept (no thrashing). Allies (same faction, loyalty) resent an attacker unless it is retaliating. Paths (path.rs) are A* over `movement::destination` (new pure version of a step; doors count open) and avoid fire and air over 55 C.

- 2026-10-10: the sea, ships, shot, long sight and liquid pressure (for Broadside, all game-agnostic; a separate session from the creature work). Mickey's brief: waves, occasional whirlpools, currents and tides all present, but no need to model every square inch of water.
  - `sea.rs` `Sea`: fields, not cells. Bathymetry at `res` m (from a fn, or `Sea::from_atlas`). Tide = M2 + S2, so springs and neaps; `stream()` floods while the tide rises and is slack at high and low water. Currents = steady + tidal far-field velocity, bent round the coast by a depth-weighted potential flow forced by *transport* (in across the upstream edge, out the downstream one; forcing by potential along every edge fed water in along the sides, so straits did not race). Solved once by multigrid V-cycles in f64 (a 1024^2 sea takes ~0.3 s; plain SOR took 23 s and f32 rounding stalled it). Waves = one 10-frequency x 16-direction spectrum: the wind grows bins toward a fetch-limited JONSWAP sea (an hour for 8 s waves), and bins it no longer feeds decay as swell (about a day for 10 s waves). The surface is a sum of up to 48 sinusoids, scaled locally for shelter (fetch to the land upwind, ≤5 km), shoaling and depth-limited breaking (`breaking()` = surf). Whirlpools are vortices that turn, draw in and dip the surface; they are shed at headlands (`sites`) when the race runs hard and fall still at slack water, and games can add lasting ones (`Whirlpool::new`). `look()`/`draw()` render a chart at any metres per cell, rotated for ship-up.
  - `craft.rs` `Craft`: a ship is her own `Scene`, bow toward -y, posed on the sea (x, y, heading, heave, pitch, roll). Her grid never rotates. `Craft::new` finds the hull envelope per level (cells the outside cannot reach), the waterplane and the draft for her mass, and adds a keel foil. A `Foil` (square_sail / fore_and_aft / rudder / keel) gives a normal force with a smooth stall plus friction, from air or water flow relative to the foil's own motion. Hull resistance is ITTC friction plus wave-making that climbs past hull speed. Heave, pitch and roll are damped oscillators on the sampled sea (roll period from beam and GM), and the wind heels her. Each tick the open columns of her grid outside the hull and under the keel (`col_keel`) are filled to the local sea surface, so the sea stands along her side and comes over a low rail. Shot holes (`take_hits`, `plug`) leak by Torricelli between the heads either side; the water in her is weighed and she settles, or founders. A tight deck keeps a flooded hold afloat, waterlogged; an open hatch sinks her. Also `collide()`, `draw()` (her view, plus the sea and other ships sampled in sea space beyond her grid and in her margin) and `draw_chart()`. Call `sea.step` once per tick, then `craft.step(&sea, wind)`, which also ticks her scene with the apparent wind.
  - `ballistics.rs`: `Placement` (any grid posed in sea space), `Shot` (gravity plus sphere drag through Mach 1; the quadratic-drag step is exact, so it is stable in water), and `Ballistics::step(dt, sea, wind, bodies)` with a 3D DDA through each `Body`. A wall soaks up strength x 6e5 J/m^3 (oak ≈ its crushing strength) x shot area x path; floors are 0.12 m slabs. `Damage` accumulates per cell (~12 heavy holes break 1 m of oak). Creatures and blocking furniture are reported as `HitThing`. Below ~6.5 degrees a ball ricochets off the water (Johnson's rule); steeper, it carries on under water for a few metres and can hole a hull below the waterline. Shells: `.shell(Blast, fuse)` uses `projectile::explode`. `elevation_for` / `range_at` / `trajectory` lay guns (a 24-pounder ranges ~2 km at 5 degrees).
  - `sight.rs` `look(eye, target, air, magnification, ground)`: the earth's curve with refraction (a 5 km horizon from 1.7 m, 21 km from 30 m), high ground, obstacles, wave crests (they raise the horizon and hide the bottom ~0.6 Hs of distant things), Koschmieder haze plus murk patches (smoke, fog banks, rain), and a contrast threshold by angular size and light. A glass magnifies size, not contrast. Returns `hidden_below`, `hull_down` (needs `Target::hull`), `clarity`. `skyline()` for drawing a glass view.
  - `sim/fluid.rs` `pressure()` (scratch in `Sim::pressure`): each body of brim-full cells acts as a pipe, and the highest free surface feeds the lowest opening, at most half the head difference per move. U-tubes level and flooded holds fill to the waterline. Budget 10k cells per tick. It costs ~+0.6 ms on the sandbox, because the canal now also flows through its depth (one move per tick, ~13 units).
  - `examples/seaway`: a brig and a second brig on an island-and-strait sea with a maelstrom; a/d helm, w/s sail, [ ] brace, q/e broadsides, Tab chart, o lookout aloft. Headless: `--snapshot f.html [--chart --mpc 48 --ticks N --fire]`. ~3.9 ms/tick for two ships plus the sea, ~5 ms to draw.
  - Tests: sea (9), craft (10), ballistics (10), sight (7), pressure (3); everything passes.
  - Next for Broadside: sail handling as acted work aloft, shot drawn in flight and splashes as effects, sight obstacles built from the other ships automatically, a regional wind field feeding the sea (it takes one wind now), and wiring hits on creatures to `Life` (splinters).
